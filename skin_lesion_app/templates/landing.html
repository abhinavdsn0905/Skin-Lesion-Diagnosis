About the Project

What is this research about?

Skin cancer is one of the most common malignancies worldwide. Detecting hazardous lesions like melanoma at an early stage significantly improves patient survival rates. However, visual evaluation through dermoscopy is challenging because early malignant skin spots often share visual similarities with harmless moles.

This project evaluates whether modern AI systems can reliably distinguish between seven distinct types of skin lesions. Specifically, we investigate whether state-of-the-art Vision Foundation Models (DINOv2) pretrained without human supervision can outperform industry-standard Convolutional Neural Networks (ResNet-50 and EfficientNet-B0) when evaluated under strict, lesion-aware validation protocols.

Disease Taxonomy

The 7 Diagnostic Categories Explained

Our models classify dermoscopic images into these seven medical classes defined in the HAM10000 dataset.

High Risk · Malignant
mel

Melanoma

The most aggressive and dangerous type of skin cancer. Early detection is vital for surgical removal and curative prognosis.

1,113 dataset samples
High Risk · Malignant
bcc

Basal Cell Carcinoma

The most common form of skin cancer. Rarely metastasizes, but can cause substantial local tissue destruction if untreated.

514 dataset samples
High Risk · Pre-cancerous
akiec

Actinic Keratoses

Pre-cancerous crusty lesions caused by sun exposure that can transform into invasive squamous cell carcinomas.

327 dataset samples
Low Risk · Benign
nv

Melanocytic Nevi

Common benign moles composed of pigment-producing melanocyte cells. Represents the dominant majority in populations.

6,705 dataset samples
Low Risk · Benign
bkl

Benign Keratosis

Non-cancerous skin growths like seborrheic keratoses, solar lentigines, and lichen-planus-like keratoses.

1,099 dataset samples
Low Risk · Benign
df

Dermatofibroma

Harmless fibrous nodules beneath the skin surface, often reacting after minor trauma or insect bites.

115 dataset samples
Medium Risk · Vascular
vasc

Vascular Lesions

Benign or reactive blood-vessel proliferations including angiomas, pyogenic granulomas, and telangiectasias.
