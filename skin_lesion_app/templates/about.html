🔬 Project Overview

This project develops and evaluates deep-learning models for the automated classification of dermoscopic skin-lesion images into seven diagnostic categories defined by the HAM10000 dataset (Human Against Machine with 10,000 training images).

Three model architectures are compared: a CNN baseline (ResNet-50), a lightweight efficient network (EfficientNet-B0), and a modern vision-foundation model (DINOv2) evaluated both as frozen feature extractor and with partial fine-tuning.

The goal is to explore how self-supervised vision-transformer features compare to supervised CNN baselines on a clinically relevant, class-imbalanced medical imaging task.

🧠 Methodology

1

Data Preparation

Lesion-aware train/val/test splitting ensuring no patient appears in multiple splits. Class-weighted sampling and augmentation (flips, rotations, color jitter) to address imbalance.

2

CNN Baselines

ResNet-50 and EfficientNet-B0 pretrained on ImageNet, with final classification head replaced and fine-tuned end-to-end using AdamW and class-weighted cross-entropy loss.

3

DINOv2 Evaluation

Facebook's DINOv2-base evaluated first as a frozen feature extractor (CLS token → logistic regression), then with partial fine-tuning of the last 2 transformer blocks.

4

Evaluation Metrics

Test accuracy, balanced accuracy, macro-F1, and macro-AUROC. Per-class metrics and confusion matrices used to diagnose where each model fails.

5

Explainability

Grad-CAM visualizations for CNN models and DINOv2 attention maps to qualitatively assess whether models focus on clinically relevant lesion regions.

⚠️ Limitations & Disclaimer

🏥
This is an educational research prototype, not a clinically validated diagnostic system. Predictions are softmax scores, not calibrated disease probabilities. Always consult a qualified dermatologist for any clinical evaluation.
  • HAM10000 is class-imbalanced; rare classes have few test examples and results may not reflect true clinical performance.
  • Results are from a held-out split of the same dataset — they do not establish generalization to other hospitals, cameras, skin tones, or phone photographs.
  • DINOv2 attention and Grad-CAM are qualitative attribution tools, not proof of causal clinical reasoning.
  • Softmax scores are not calibrated probabilities of disease.
  • Checkpoint selection procedures were not perfectly uniform across all models.
